### [00:01:52–00:10:33] Prologue: Hanarr on the East Road
- [00:01:52] A week before the Council of Bree, a Greenway caravan travelled the East Road. **Bill Butterbur** rode in the fourth wagon, normally kept for valuable cargo. This time it carried Hornblower leaf, the best pipe-weed of the Southfarthing.
- [00:02:35] Desperate, hungry brigands burst from the bushes and attacked the caravan.
- [00:03:35] **Hanarr**, a dwarf hired to guard Bill, carried a huge battle-axe almost too big for him, with a maul strapped to his back. He had a long beard and long hair going grey.
- [00:04:33] Hanarr muttered "I've been waiting for a fight," let out a scream, went wild-eyed in a rage, and charged to intercept the attackers.
- [00:05:04] The brigands broke at the sight of him. Some fled, and a couple surrendered to Hanarr and the other guards. The prisoners were put in the last wagon, the wounded were tended and the horses calmed.
- [00:05:46] At camp that night, travellers whispered about Hanarr. Some stared and others carefully looked away.
- [00:06:29] Bill invited him to sit by the fire: "Pretending I didn't see that would insult us both. What happened out there?" Hanarr read him (Insight 11) and guessed it was about the rage.
- [00:07:36] Hanarr deflected: a lone dwarf out here does what he must to keep himself and his employers alive.
- [00:08:10] Bill reflected that half the world is stranger than any myth, and handed Hanarr a neatly folded letter with a seal he didn't recognize (History 11).
- [00:09:56] Bill invited him to a council meeting: "not soldiers, not lords, not experts, but people who've seen things." After today, Hanarr might be "exactly the sort of person myself and Bree will need."

## Style 3: Chronicle of the West

*Being an account of how four strangers came together at Bree in the year 2981 of the Third Age*

[00:00:57] In those days the world was changing, and few marked it. Folk tended their fields and minded their own corners of Middle-earth, while beyond the hearth-light old shadows stirred and forgotten things came to light again.

[00:01:52] A week before the Council of Bree, a caravan of the Greenway Company wound along the East Road beneath a grey sky. In its fourth wagon rode Master Bill Butterbur, and with him a cargo of Hornblower leaf, the finest the Southfarthing grows. When hungry men burst from the thickets, it was the dwarf Hanarr who met them, axe in hand and a cry of battle on his lips, his eyes gone wild. The brigands broke before him like chaff. That night by the fire Bill spoke plainly: to pretend he had not seen would insult them both. Hanarr gave little away, but Bill pressed a sealed letter into his hand and bade him come to Bree, for, he said, he had need of folk who had seen things.

[00:10:33] Far to the west in Longbottom, the house of Hornblower was busy with leaf and ledgers and quarrels about wagon wheels. Nihla Hornblower heard her father say the business in Bree was too weighty for her, and that it should go to Odo, who had once lost a whole wagon. So when the postman came up the lane with a letter from Bree, she took it herself, read it in her room, and before long was riding in the back of a neighbour's cart, farther from home than she had ever been.

[00:21:03] East of Bree, with the High Pass behind them, the elf Thalion walked beside Aldrin, son of Borin, ranger and teacher. A weary rider overtook them with Bill's letter. Aldrin read it, and remembered aloud how Thalion had come to him after eight years of captivity knowing only how to survive. "Answers rarely wait where we left them," he said, and sent Thalion on to Bree alone.

[00:27:30] And in Bree itself, on a night of wind and rain, Osric Thistlewool set down his whetstone at a sound at his door. No one was in the lane. But on his step lay a letter on costly paper, perfectly dry, bearing his own name.

[00:34:08] So it was that on council day the four met in a side room of the Council Hall, strangers to one another. The hobbit bristled at the tall folk. The dwarf ate the cured meats. The man of Bree sat very straight. The elf, taller than any door was built for, tuned his lute poorly and grew cross with it. Nihla boasted of her family's fame and fumbled the telling, but she gave Osric a pouch of her leaf all the same, and when she looked at last into Thalion's face she saw both the fair elf of the old tales and a sorrow that grieved her.

[00:57:15] The Council of Bree was held in a half-round hall under Mayor Coombe. Five councillors sat with Bill, and the Butterbur's guests sat behind him. Three reports were read: a footprint far larger than any man's in the Weather Hills; caravans harried on the Greenway by what seemed to be scouts; farms abandoned beneath barrows where lights were seen at night. The council argued and could not agree. Then a voice from the crowd said that the wild had grown restless, and that more things pass through every door than most folk know. No one could say who had spoken. Hanarr had already noticed an old man in a blue cloak, knitting in a corner and trying not to be seen. Then a road warden burst in from the rain: the last survivor of a Greenway caravan was dead, and had spoken only two words, again and again: *seeing* and *listening*.

[01:25:00] While the council raged, Bill led the four aside and gave them twenty gold apiece, more than a mercenary would ask. They felt that he knew more than he said. Still, they agreed to hear him out at the Greenway House that evening.

[01:52:29] There, in a house whose public rooms seemed strangely small for its size, Bill laid out every trouble he knew, and one more besides: records had been stolen from the archives of Bree. The company argued long. Thalion would ride east against the orcs; Hanarr felt a pull he could not name toward the haunted south; Nihla and Osric went with the dwarf. In the end the hobbit laid her hand on the elf's shoulder and believed very hard, and Thalion gave way, though not happily, for he judged that Aldrin had sent him here to learn something.

[02:30:33] That night in the Prancing Pony, the old man in the blue cloak sat knitting by the fire, a small blue scarf with stars worked into it. Hanarr sat down beside him and asked what he wanted, and got only riddles. As the dwarf rose to go, the old man said that all roads are threads, if one looks at them. Thalion looked him in the eye and told him that they were about to change things, and he might as well share what he knew. What answer came, the tale does not tell.
