### Scene 1: Dolls in the dark [00:09:59–00:23:30]
Around midnight, **Hanarr**, **Thalion** and **Nihla** walked back from the raided barrow to the farmhouse, where **Osric** had bedded down in the least conspicuous corner he could find. They found him fast asleep under a pile of the children's dolls, their faces turned toward him (the DM shared a picture). Neither Hanarr (Insight 10) nor Thalion (Insight 5) could tell whether he had arranged them himself or whether they had moved. Hanarr barged in: "Hey, Osric! There wasn't even any scary stuff in there!" and debriefed him. Osric woke with a start: "Why would you cover me in dolls like this, mate? I was trying to rest!" Hanarr swore it was like that when he came in ("I thought you just liked the comfort of dolls"), and Osric decided it must have been Thalion. Osric pushed the dolls off his legs, and the DM noted they ended up back on the ledge. Osric admitted the lights had been unlike anything he'd seen, and that "Thalion and Missy over there" were doing some pretty weird things too. Put off and a little spooked ("maybe this guy's weird"), Hanarr went to sleep in a doll-free corner, saying he needed rest to bring his anger out again. Thalion dragged a chair into the corner and sat watching everyone all night. "Strange times," said Osric. "Maybe I'd rather the dolls," said Hanarr, before asking to be woken with a pebble: he's quick with the axe. Thalion wondered whether whatever moved the dolls was tied to the ghosts, but the night stayed quiet and no doll moved.

### Scene 2: The Queen of the Stars [00:24:26–00:38:49]
In the middle of the night, Nihla heard something and looked out: a woman stood on the road leading away from the farmhouse, looking up at the night sky. Thalion, awake in his chair, saw Nihla rise and look out, but saw nothing outside himself. Nihla felt a tug toward the woman, a good and familiar one, like the one she had felt when her light came. The woman was beautiful, in a dark greyish robe, with dark hair and a glow about her; her hair seemed made of tiny stars, and she looked elven. "Do you feel that?" Nihla asked Thalion. "I don't." "Just going out for a wee." Outside, though she was too far away to hear a voice, Nihla heard one anyway. The woman pointed at a lantern on the porch and snapped its light out. "Show me the road. I cannot see it." Nihla took a huge breath: "I guess I wasn't crazy after all. I knew you were there. You've been the one to show me the way before. I'd be lost without you. And now you need me to show you?" "Yes, Nihla. Show me the road." Nihla wept a little, from relief and from belief: for the first time this thing was tangible. "But I do not know the way." "The light is mine," the woman said. "It can avail you in many places. The choice to carry it is yours." "I've been carrying your light all my life, I think." "Yes. I was with you the whole way." Nihla asked whether this was her adventure or the company's. "It is yours, Nihla. I've chosen you for a very special reason. You asked my name. The elves have given me many names. One is **Varda**." The DM added that she is known as the Queen of the Stars, a name Nihla did not know (a high-DC History check). "Was it you today who helped me wield this light? Can I do that again?" Yes. Nihla turned away for a moment to think, and when she turned back the woman was gone and the lantern was lit again. Nihla clutched the pendant she had made long ago out of scraps and bits, and ran back to the house: "I guess this is the start of something bigger than I."

### Scene 3: A tale for a sleepy hobbit [00:39:08–00:43:15]
Nihla came back in to find Thalion sitting bolt upright with his eyes open. She remembered him going on about "Elbereth" the night before, but the name didn't connect for her with the woman who had just introduced herself. Having smashed a raven with a mace that morning and badly needing sleep, she asked the elf for a bedtime story: "I haven't met an elf before, and to be quite frank I've heard some tall tales from some very short people." Thalion told her the tale of **Lúthien Tinúviel**, a young elf who danced beautifully in the woods by herself, and Nihla fell asleep. (The table agreed Lúthien was dark-haired, not blonde.) Everyone woke rested from a long rest.
- **Clues:** [00:40:15] Nihla hasn't linked Thalion's Elbereth to Varda
- **Lore:** [00:41:56] the tale of Lúthien Tinúviel

### Scene 4: The morning song [00:43:15–00:49:12]
Hanarr was up and ready: west to the Barrow-downs, "that guy was real concerned, and we're eight hours behind." Nihla checked how Osric's wounds were healing ("Sorry, cold hands"). Osric wondered whether she had weird abilities that sped up healing overnight; he had never seen purple light glowing out of people before. "I'm not sure that weird is the right word, sir," said Nihla. The dolls had not moved. "Dolls feel if someone's watching," said Thalion, "and someone was watching." Before they left, Nihla gave Thalion a meaningful glance, took out her newly bought lute, and plucked and sang a song as a secret payment for his story, giving the company Heroic Inspiration for the day. Hanarr grabbed a dry ration: "What are we waiting for? Let's hit the road." Thalion appreciated the inspiration but felt a twinge: who was she? On the road, Thalion rode up front with a head start and quietly tried to play Nihla's tune, badly, hoping nobody would hear; she rode up with unexpected patience to offer tips, and he put the lute away the moment she got close. Osric asked Hanarr about his home. Hanarr said he wasn't well travelled and had left his mountain only months ago, and promised that next time he meant something cultural he'd say so, "like the helmets."
- **Clues:** [00:44:55] Osric saw Nihla's light as purple; [00:45:18] the dolls stay still when watched
- **Items:** Heroic Inspiration from Nihla's song [00:45:24]

### Scene 5: Tracking across the downs [00:49:12–00:53:14]
An hour's ride brought them back to the first barrow, and from there they turned west, as the spirit had pointed. Thalion (17) and Osric (Survival 19) picked out the direction of the footprints and the broken brush. Hanarr realized that the fresh prints from the floating figure in the field had led to the barrow, and others went in and out of it, so the "spirit" they had seen had a physical presence. The Barrow-downs were rolling, deadish hills and mounds with little forest, and the tracking took three hours under an overcast sky. Then a bigger hill rose ahead with a clearer trail leading to the entrance of a barrow, larger and more ornate than the first: **seven stars** carved over its opening, a busted door, and fresh footprints leading in. "Bigger is better," said Hanarr, climbing off his pony. He reasoned that whoever hadn't found the scroll in the first barrow had come here to ransack this one, and planned to look for the same kind of hiding spot.

- **Clues:** [00:51:00] the pale figure left footprints, so it was physical; [00:52:10] seven stars over the door; [00:52:27] fresh footprints going in
- **Rolls:** Thalion 17, Osric Survival 19 [00:49:24–00:49:36]

### Scene 6: Scholars from Tharbad [00:53:14–01:12:15]
The mound was perhaps sixty feet long, no labyrinth. Nihla handed out torches, put her mace away and lowered her shield. The group Stealth check scraped through (Hanarr rolled a natural 1 at disadvantage in his armour and simply put his helmet on; two of four succeeded). Ten feet inside, in the gloom, stood two rough-looking men with coats and big beards, alive and moving, poking through side burial alcoves. Hanarr spent his Heroic Inspiration on Perception: their boots matched the tracks from the first barrow. The five-foot doorway opened into a chamber some thirty feet wide, and the party had the surprise. Thalion put a hand on Hanarr's shoulder to signal silently. The man in the coat was telling his companion, "Very interesting things here." Nihla felt no hostile vibes, but Hanarr distrusted them: they had broken in, ransacked, and the ghost had warned that "they search again." Nihla called it the plot of Hamlet: do you trust the ghost or not? "I trust the ghost," said Thalion. Non-lethal, they agreed. A round of Perception (Thalion 9, Nihla 14, Osric 15, Hanarr 14) showed four people: the leader, an assistant with glasses writing in a book, and two guards. Osric slipped into a dark corner. Thalion boomed, "Halt! This is our tomb." "Oh, hello there. I didn't notice you," said the leader. "Of course not. We were watching you walking in." Hanarr introduced himself: sent by Bill of the Greenway Company with Bree's authority to investigate happenings in these parts. "Bree, you say? I know someone from Bree. His name is **Crowmantle**. You know Crowmantle? He works for us." When Hanarr answered that Bill Butterbur sat higher on the council than their Crowmantle, the man said he knew no Bill Butterbur, and besides, they were not from Bree: "We're scholars from **Tharbad**." Insight (Nihla 12, Hanarr 13, Thalion 20) said the "scholar" was a rough, callous man, though his bespectacled assistant might be the real one. Around them lay crowbars, chisels, parchments and maps, and no visible weapons. Thalion asked what they were looking for. Nihla, standing as tall as she could, demanded to see their permit to dig the barrows (Deception); the man said permits weren't a thing out here. Thalion nodded and rested his hand on his sword hilt. "I've been at this for twenty years," the leader said. "Let's stand down a little. Maybe we can work together. We're just trying to restore the history of Cardolan." Thalion's Intimidation (22) rolled off the guards. The door, Hanarr noted, had been forced and battered: "Looks like you forced your way in, for academics." "They're hard to break into, but there's important history in here we need to find." "And you're messing it up?" said Thalion. "This is ours. You broke our door, and now you're looking through our stuff." "So you're a descendant of the people buried here?" "Absolutely not. This is the land of Bree." "I beg to differ, sir. Bree doesn't own this land." In the tense pause, Hanarr asked for a moment to consult with his compatriots ("Very well. Meanwhile we continue our work"). Hanarr's plan: plant a scrap of Thalion's parchment in the old lead tube, let the raiders "find" it and leave, then find the real thing. But the leader had already taken a crowbar to one of the tombs.

### Scene 7: Specters [01:12:15–02:06:06]
When the crowbar cracked the tomb open, a puff of smoke burst out, and three **specters** rose from it and attacked the raiders at once. (Nihla and Osric both rolled 19 for initiative; Nihla took the first turn.) The DM confirmed that drawing blood on humans unprovoked could cost Shadow points. Nihla put her hand on her pendant, thought hard about the person who had spoken to her in the night, and cast **Bless** on Hanarr, Thalion and Osric. Osric slipped back into the corridor (Stealth 25). The specters were black-faced things with empty cavities for eyes, and they ignored the party entirely. Thalion agonized over whether to stab an untrustworthy scholar in the back ("Tom Bombadil broke this open," he argued, until the DM told the table to stop metagaming and play their characters). "What do we do?" he asked his friends, and did nothing but ready Blade Ward (his Insight was a natural 1). The leader swung an axe twice at a specter and it shrieked. Then the specters reached out: two touched the leader and one touched a guard. Hanarr (Perception 18) and Thalion (20) saw a black spot spreading on the leader's left hand under his glove, the same mark as on the old man with the cart. The man screamed, "Help me! Help us!" "They're the source of the problem," said Nihla, "but I don't think they deserve to die." Hanarr drew his greataxe for non-lethal blows, raged ("Don't desecrate tombs!"), hit the leader (17 with Bless) for 10 slashing, and cleaved into a guard, bloodying him: "You deceptive people, ransacking graves. You don't deserve anything." The guards attacked the specters, and one guard destroyed the first. "This is the DM trying to show us the scholars are not our enemies!" said Thalion. Nihla dropped her torch (a hole in the ceiling let in some light), and cast **Inflict Wounds** on a specter, which was immune and didn't even acknowledge her. Osric sneak-attacked a specter with his short sword (22 to hit, 18 damage, resisted) and followed with a natural 20 on his scimitar that left it flickering. "The ghosts are on our side! What are you doing?" cried Thalion. "Casper, no!" added Hanarr. Thalion frightened one guard, then attacked the leader non-lethally and bloodied him. Osric told the leader to stand down and leave; the man glanced at him and kept fighting the specter. Hanarr hit the leader again (26 to hit, 8 slashing) and cleaved at the bloodied guard. The guards broke and ran, and Hanarr's opportunity attack with the flat of his greataxe across the face knocked one out cold, while Nihla's torch swing missed. Nihla cast **Spare the Dying** on the unconscious guard, who was already stable ("I wanted to do something cool"). Osric cut at a specter (12 slashing, resisted) and then landed a 27 for a single point of damage. Thalion struck the leader with the back of his curved blade: 12 radiant damage, and the leader went down. He told the remaining guard to get out of there; Hanarr roared the same. A specter clawed that guard for 10 and his arm turned black; he too had the mark on his left hand. Hanarr swung his maul and toppled him (26 to hit, Constitution save 13 against DC 14, 7 bludgeoning, prone). The guard scrambled up, screamed, and ran, and Hanarr knocked him out. Nihla cast Spare the Dying on a downed guard, and Osric, with his luck, destroyed the last specter: the ghost slayer.

### Scene 8: Ropes, loot and a journal [02:06:06–02:20:39]
Still raging, Hanarr dragged the unconscious men toward the door. Thalion pulled out his rope and told the assistant, "We're going to tie you up now." "Okay, sir. I don't want no trouble." Thalion, who has three fifty-foot ropes and no training in knots, tied all three with Nihla's help and stripped their weapons. Osric looked the prisoners over. He didn't recognize the leader. As far as Osric knew, Crowmantle was an upstanding citizen of the Bree council. Tharbad, though, he knew of: a ruined town full of thugs, where dangerous people and things are taken because nobody polices it, and the thuggery there is run by a man called **Thurno**. The leader's "scholars from Tharbad" had been either a slip or overconfidence. Osric opened the chest the raiders had cracked: twelve ancient-looking silver pennies, a silver ring worth about 5 gold, an ornate cloak clasp worth about 7½, and a vial of blue liquid. Hanarr sat in a corner breathing hard to keep his rage going. Osric readied a dash, snatched the clasp and bolted for the corridor; nothing happened, so he pocketed it and reported that there was treasure after all. "I have no need for a dead man's things," said Thalion. "But the dead don't need them either," said Osric. Nihla told him to pocket what he liked. Thalion's first goal was to fix the curse, though he didn't know where it came from; Nihla's answer was that these bastards opening tombs let the ghosts out. The DM reminded them that their mission was to find answers, and that clues could be carried off for later study. Hanarr wanted the hidden compartment, and the assistant questioned. Nihla played good cop. The assistant said he had been hired only a week ago; a lot of men had come up from Tharbad, and his job was to find things and produce a report. Thalion read his journal. It recorded the last barrow: they broke in, dug around for clues, had an encounter with "our" spirit, found nothing and moved on. The first entry stated their mission: find **the key**. The DM noted the file showed activity going back longer than a few days.

### Scene 9: Father Corwyn and the brotherhood [02:20:39–02:31:12]
Hanarr asked Osric for help finding a hiding spot like the first barrow's, and with Osric helping and Nihla's Guidance he rolled an Investigation of 14: a mechanism at the front of the chamber opened a door onto a secret room, one the raiders would have found had the party come any later. Before going in, Nihla used her bandages to bring the leader round. "Oh. The ghosts are gone." "It's about time we get the truth out of you, sir," she said. "We've been sent to investigate apparitions and ghosts, and lo and behold, you open a box and out come the ghosts. How long have you been at this? And you can bet your sweet bottom you won't be doing it again." Their job, he said, was to find a key; to what, he didn't know. He had received word in Tharbad. From whom? "If I tell you, it'll be the death of me." (Osric stepped away so as not to hear.) "We received word from **Father Corwyn** that we were to find a key in these barrows, and to look for the barrow with seven stars on it." He had a sketch of the key. Nihla pulled up his sleeve: "What does this mean?" The mark was seven small black stars, not arranged like those of Arnor, the Greenway seal or the knitter's scarf. "Life is hard out in the wild. This is the mark of a brotherhood." "A dark brotherhood." "You should really try and get your money back for this," said Nihla. Thalion asked whether the brotherhood was its name. "No. The name is sacred." Could he write it down? "I can't spell it." Nihla (18) judged he had given what he was willing to give, and nothing more was worth it to him. Osric asked whether to let them go; Thalion said no, they would be taken to the city and interrogated. Hanarr offered to stay outside and guard them, hoping one would try to escape. Osric, stung that nobody trusted him alone with the prisoners, stayed with Hanarr. "He feels for these guys," said Thalion. "I told you."

### Scene 10: The altar of seven stars [02:31:12–02:37:36]
Thalion and Nihla went into the secret chamber with a torch. The walls were covered in carvings, and three or four pedestals held bowls of bluish liquid, like the vial's. Nihla lit them, and the room glowed. Two half-torn banners hung there: one with the familiar tree and a few stars, another with stars. In the centre stood a round altar some fifteen feet across, ringed with seven stars, and carved in its middle a noble of Arnor holding what looked like a stone. Around the altar's edge ran Arnorian lettering and dials that could be turned, with seven stars set at different positions. "Nihla, would you give me a hand? I think we have to place the stars correctly, like on the flag we saw." "That's been coming up a lot. I've pretty much got it down pat." Together they turned the stars into the pattern of the Greenway seal and the Arnorian flag, and a heavy thump sounded.

### Scene 11: The noble's tomb [02:37:37–02:55:02]
The thump came from a tomb at the far end of the room, whose lid had swung open on its own. Thalion walked up slowly, watching for traps and respectful; Nihla rolled a 5 looking for them and called Osric in to check. "Thalion seems to be handling that," he said, but came. Something dripped on Thalion's head: only humidity. He pushed the heavy lid aside. Inside lay a skeleton in decrepit noble armour with a ruined helmet, and several little chests buried beside him as mementos. Osric wanted to understand: "I thought we were lifting a curse, but now we seem to be doing the same thing these guys were doing before we got here, and for some reason it was wrong for them but not wrong for us. I don't quite understand the logic of the elves." "I didn't break any doors," said Thalion. "This coffin opened before me, and I looked in it." "And we're following the stars," said Nihla. "It can't be a bad thing." Thalion: their actions followed the council's orders, which meant undoing the raiders'; he was looking for any clue to how the curse began. Hanarr held that the spirit had trusted them to find the thing, and that if they didn't, more men would be sent. (The DM added that the thugs were higher-level hired men acting on instructions, that more would come, and that the barrows had lain undisturbed for hundreds of years.) The little chests were untrapped. They held an ancient ceremonial crown; a big **star sapphire**; a **scimitar** in a scabbard that glowed when drawn (Osric: "I'll take the scimitar, for sure"); a leaf-bound book of blank pages; an amulet with a mechanism inside; a **longsword** of a metal nobody recognized, which Thalion examined; a glass jar of liquid that smelled of coffee; and a container holding a brass **key**. "It's interesting," said the DM, "that you guys ended up with that key." Osric would take a short rest to attune to the scimitar, and Hanarr, outside, wanted a look at the amulet.

### Scene 12: The seeing stone [02:55:02–03:05:29]
With the thump, a small cavity had also dropped open in the centre of the altar, holding a rusted lead tube like the first, but better preserved. Its parchment read: *"The seeing stone was not sent north with the others. Following the Witch-king's advance, the eye was entrusted to the house of the wardens. May the seven stars conceal the road."* "I suppose there was a **palantír** in the middle of the altar," said Thalion. Nobody could say whether this was the other half of the first parchment. Nihla put the tube in Osric's pack ("We got the key!") and went through the raiders' bags for food: dry rations and a cake-like loaf, which she ate. Osric took his short rest to attune. Then the question of the prisoners: the horses were with them, three hours' ride from the farm, and Bree lay about a day's ride away, or two days on foot with three bound men to walk. They would sleep with the bandits tied up and march them to Bree. "If they leave, we're in shit," said Thalion. At the end of the session the DM identified the finds: a **Moon-touched scimitar**, an **adamantine longsword**, a **clockwork amulet** (once a day, take a 10 instead of rolling; no attunement), and a bottle of pristine old Arnorian coffee, a recipe nobody has anymore. The company reached **level 2**.
